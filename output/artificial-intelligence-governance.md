# Artificial Intelligence Governance: Literature Synthesis

*Research report on: artificial intelligence governance*

## Outline

### Introduction
- This report reviews 3 papers on artificial intelligence governance published between 2021 and 2022: "Creative Problem Solving in Artificially Intelligent Agents: A Survey and Framework" (Gizzi, 2022); "The Artificial Scientist: Logicist, Emergentist, and Universalist Approaches to Artificial General Intelligence" (Bennett, 2021); "Compression, The Fermi Paradox and Artificial Super-Intelligence" (Bennett, 2021).
- Recurring themes across these works include agents, emergence and facilitate.
- The papers appeared in arXiv.

### Literature Review
- The aim of this survey is to provide a comprehensive overview of the current state-of-the-art (SOTA) in creative problem solving (CPS) within artificially intelligent autonomous systems.
- The survey will examine different approaches, including those that focus on off-nominal problems and those that deal with environmental uncertainty through creativity.
- This paper explores the concept of an artificial scientist as a logical, emergentist, or universalist approach to constructing AGI.
- It discusses various approaches that may facilitate this, including those that are based on a unified or hybrid theory for AGI's requirements.
- The paper concludes that a unified or hybrid theory is necessary and explores two theories that satisfy this requirement to some degree.

### Methodology
- This review retrieved papers from arxiv with the query "artificial intelligence governance" and compared the 3 most relevant by theme and research design.
- The reviewed papers fall into the following research designs: surveys or reviews of prior work (Gizzi, 2022); analytical discussions (Bennett, 2021; Bennett, 2021).
- Non-empirical designs such as reviews and frameworks map the field and set out principles, but they offer limited direct evidence of how well those principles work in practice.

### Discussion/Findings
- Read together, the papers converge on agents and emergence as the issues that matter most for artificial intelligence governance.
- The emergence of increasingly autonomous systems dictates the necessity for AI agents to deal with environmental uncertainty through creativity (Gizzi, 2022).
- Communication then requires two agents labour under similar compulsions and have similar experiences (construct similar solutions to similar tasks) (Bennett, 2021).

### Research Gaps & Future Work
- Empirical validation
- Integration across research strands
- Generalisability across contexts

### Conclusion
- Across the 3 papers reviewed, research on artificial intelligence governance centres on agents, emergence and facilitate.
- The evidence comes from analytical discussions and surveys or reviews of prior work, so the field's conclusions rest on a mixed methodological base.
- Together these works indicate that artificial intelligence governance remains an active area where the open questions identified in this review warrant further study.

## Literature Synthesis

The 3 papers reviewed, published between 2021 and 2022, approach artificial intelligence governance from different angles: Gizzi (2022) examines creative problem solving in artificially intelligent agents, Bennett (2021) examines the artificial scientist and Bennett (2021) examines compression, the fermi paradox and artificial Super-Intelligence. Despite these different starting points, they share a vocabulary of agents, emergence and facilitate, which marks these as the concerns that connect otherwise separate lines of work.

Methodologically the work is varied, spanning surveys or reviews of prior work (Gizzi, 2022) and analytical discussions (Bennett, 2021; Bennett, 2021). This mix means the papers support each other only indirectly: their claims rest on different kinds of evidence and are rarely evaluated against one another.

Taken together, the literature treats agents as a central concern for artificial intelligence governance, but it leaves several questions open, which are described under Research Gaps & Future Work.

## Research Gaps & Future Work

- **Empirical validation**: Most of the reviewed work consists of analytical discussions and surveys or reviews of prior work (Gizzi, 2022; Bennett, 2021; Bennett, 2021) rather than real-world evaluation. Whether the proposed approaches actually work in practice for artificial intelligence governance is therefore largely untested. Deployment studies and measurable outcome data are needed to confirm or refute their claims.
- **Integration across research strands**: The papers study creative problem solving in artificially intelligent agents, the artificial scientist and compression, the fermi paradox and artificial Super-Intelligence largely in isolation, each with its own framing and evidence. There is little work connecting these strands, so it is unclear how progress in one affects the others. A unifying framework or comparative study for artificial intelligence governance would make the existing findings easier to build on.
- **Generalisability across contexts**: Each reviewed paper draws its conclusions from a specific setting, and none compares several settings side by side. It is therefore unclear whether the findings transfer to other countries, sectors or populations, or whether they still hold given developments since 2022. Cross-context replication would show which results are general and which are local.

## Paper Summaries

### Creative Problem Solving in Artificially Intelligent Agents: A Survey and Framework
*Evana Gizzi, Lakshmi Nair, Sonia Chernova, Jivko Sinapov (2022)*

Creative Problem Solving (CPS) is a sub-area within Artificial Intelligence (AI) that focuses on methods for solving off-nominal, or anomalous problems in autonomous systems. To stimulate further research in CPS, we present a definition and a framework of CPS, which we adopt to categorize existing AI methods in this field.

### The Artificial Scientist: Logicist, Emergentist, and Universalist Approaches to Artificial General Intelligence
*Michael Timothy Bennett, Yoshihiro Maruyama (2021)*

We attempt to define what is necessary to construct an Artificial Scientist, explore and evaluate several approaches to artificial general intelligence (AGI) which may facilitate this, conclude that a unified or hybrid approach is necessary and explore two theories that satisfy this requirement to some degree.

### Compression, The Fermi Paradox and Artificial Super-Intelligence
*Michael Timothy Bennett (2021)*

The following briefly discusses possible difficulties in communication with and control of an AGI (artificial general intelligence), building upon an explanation of The Fermi Paradox and preceding work on symbol emergence and artificial general intelligence. The latter suggests that to infer what someone means, an agent constructs a rationale for the observed behaviour of others.
